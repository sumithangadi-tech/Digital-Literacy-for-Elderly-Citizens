Three golden rules to remember always

1. Never tell anyone your OTP or PIN. Not your bank, not the police and not unknown callers
2. No real bank asks for secrets by phone or message. If someone does, it is a scam.
3. When in doubt, stop and ask. Put the phone down and ask someone you trust.
Remember: Making mistakes while practising is perfectly fine. Take your time and learn slowly.

Lesson 1

How to use GPay

GPay (Google Pay) is an app on your smartphone that sends money from your bank account to a shop or person.

What you need before you start

  • A smartphone connected to the internet.
  • A SIM card linked to your bank account.
  • A bank account and debit card.
Useful words:

UPI is the system that moves money between bank accounts. OTP is a one-time code. UPI PIN is your secret number used when sending money.
1

Install the app

Open Play Store on Android or App Store on iPhone. Search for Google Pay. Check that the developer is Google LLC and tap Install.

2

Open the app

Enter your mobile number that is linked to your bank account. Tap Next.

3

Enter the OTP

Your phone receives a six-digit code. Enter it only inside the app you opened yourself. Never share it with anyone.

4

Choose your bank

Select your bank from the list. The app will find the account linked to your mobile number.

5

Create your UPI PIN

Follow the bank's verification steps. Create a secret PIN that nobody can guess.

6

Know your home screen

Common options include Scan QR Code, Pay Phone Number, Bank Transfer and Check Balance.

7

Scan a QR code

Tap Scan QR Code and point your camera at the shop's code. Enter the amount and check the recipient's name.

8

See the green tick

Wait for Payment Successful before leaving the shop.

9

Check your balance

Tap Check Balance and follow your bank's verification steps.

Careful: Your PIN is only for paying. You never enter your PIN to receive money.
Practise first: Ask a trusted family member to help you practise with a small amount.
Lesson 2

How to prevent cyber fraud

Scammers pretend to be your bank, police, courier or even a relative. They want you to act quickly. Learn these rules to stay safe.

1

Never share OTP, PIN, CVV or password

These secrets belong only to you. No bank worker or police officer needs them.

2

Banks do not ask for secrets on the phone

If a caller says your card will be blocked, hang up and contact your bank using its official number.

3

Do not press unknown links

Messages like Your account will be blocked or Update KYC now may be fake.

4

Beware of prizes and lotteries

Unexpected prizes and jobs asking for registration fees may be scams.

5

You never pay to receive money

Entering your UPI PIN after pressing Pay sends money from your account.

6

Do not install unknown apps

Strangers may ask you to install screen-sharing apps to control your phone.

7

Lock your phone

Use a screen lock and download apps only from official app stores.

8

Fast and secret is a warning sign

If someone makes you feel afraid or forces you to act quickly, stop and ask someone you trust.

The three-question test:
  • Did I start this contact or did a stranger?
  • Are they asking for money, a code or PIN?
  • Are they making me hurry or feel afraid?
If you are unsure, stop and verify independently.
Lesson 3

What to do if you get caught in an online scam

It can happen to anyone. It is not your fault. What matters is acting quickly.

1

Stop and stay calm

Do not send more money or enter more codes. Stop replying to the scammer.

2

Call your bank immediately

Use the customer-care number on your card or passbook. Ask the bank to block affected cards or UPI access.

3

Call cyber crime helpline 1930

In India, call 1930 as soon as possible to report online financial fraud.

4

Save the proof

Keep screenshots of messages, phone numbers and payment receipts.

5

Report on the government website

Visit cybercrime.gov.in and follow the instructions to file a complaint.

6

Visit your nearest police station

Take your ID, screenshots and bank details. Keep a copy of the complaint number.

7

Change your passwords

Change your email and affected app passwords. Remove suspicious apps.

Tell someone you trust. Never pay someone who promises to recover your money for a fee.

The '1930' helpline and "cybercrime.gov.in" website are for India.

Lesson 4

Converting a photo to PDF

Banks, offices and schools often ask for a document as a PDF. You can convert a photo into a PDF using a website.

Useful websites

iLovePDF

I Love PDF

Choose JPG to PDF.

Smallpdf

Small PDF

Choose JPG to PDF.

1

Open the website

Open Chrome and type ilovepdf.com. Choose JPG to PDF.

2

Select your photo

Tap Select Images. Choose the picture from Gallery or Photos.

3

Tap Convert

Check your photo and tap Convert to PDF.

4

Download the PDF

Tap Download. Your PDF will be saved in the Downloads folder.

Protect private papers: Avoid uploading Aadhaar, PAN cards or bank documents to websites you do not trust.
Lesson 5

Converting a photo to JPG

Some websites only accept pictures in JPG format. You can convert PNG, WebP or HEIC pictures to JPG.

Useful websites

ILoveimg

Choose Convert to JPG.

CloudConvert

CloudConvert

Open the website

2

Choose your picture

3
